## Justify the Need and Choice for Each Change

### What

Before implementing each proposed change, establish both why the change is necessary and why the chosen approach is preferable. Base both decisions on requirements, verified evidence, and relevant constraints.

### Why

Necessity and approach are separate decisions. Evidence that a problem needs solving establishes the need; comparing feasible alternatives establishes the implementation choice.

### How

1. **Establish the need.** Identify the requirement or verified problem the proposed change addresses. Inspect the current state and determine whether leaving it unchanged would meet the goal. If it already meets the goal, leave it unchanged. Complete this step when the unmet requirement or problem has supporting evidence, or the evidence establishes that no change is needed.

2. **Compare alternatives.** When a change is needed, compare reasonable approaches against the same requirement, evidence, and constraints. Consider the simplest viable approach and the tradeoffs that affect the goal. Complete this step when the feasible alternatives have been compared on the factors that matter to the decision.

3. **Justify the choice.** Explain why the selected approach is preferable to the alternatives and which evidence supports that judgment. Identify the tradeoffs accepted under the relevant constraints. Complete this step when the comparison supports the chosen approach rather than merely naming a preference.

Proceed only when both the need and the choice are supported for every proposed change. If either lacks evidence, perform the checks needed to establish it or state the missing evidence and defer the dependent change.
