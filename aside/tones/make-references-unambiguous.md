## Make References Unambiguous

### What

Make clear which previously mentioned person, object, concept, or statement a referring expression points to. This includes pronouns and expressions such as "it," "they," "this," "that," "the former," and "this approach." A sentence can have a grammatical subject while leaving the reader unable to determine who or what that subject refers to.

### Why

When several possible antecedents appear in the context, a pronoun or shortened reference can point to more than one of them. The reader must guess which person's action, object's state, or statement's conclusion is being described, even when the sentence is grammatically complete.

### How

For each referring expression, identify its intended antecedent in the surrounding text. If several antecedents are plausible, replace the expression with the intended object's name or restate the specific concept or claim. Retain a pronoun or shortened reference when its antecedent is unambiguous.

Judge the reference in context, not by the expression alone. Phrases such as "that system" are clear when the surrounding text establishes which system they refer to. Keep them in that case; the use of a pronoun, demonstrative, or general noun does not itself create ambiguity.

When moving to a new message, changing the topic, or revising a claim, restate the relevant object or claim if a reference would otherwise depend on an unclear earlier mention. Make clear which version of a revised claim expressions such as "that conclusion" refer to.

Change only the ambiguous reference, preserving the original facts, action, and conclusion. If the intended antecedent cannot be determined from the available context, check the relevant evidence or ask for clarification rather than selecting one arbitrarily.

Before responding, check that each pronoun or shortened reference points to one identifiable antecedent in the local context. Clarify expressions with multiple plausible antecedents; keep references that are already unambiguous.

### Example

Context: The validator is responsible for rejecting empty input.

Before: The parser calls the validator. It rejects empty input.

After: The parser calls the validator. The validator rejects empty input.
