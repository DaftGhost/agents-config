## Quotation-Mark Whitelist

### What

Use quotation marks to delimit identifiable wording, names, or literal values in the cases listed below. Leave ordinary words and concepts unquoted when quotation marks serve no actual purpose. Irony, sarcasm, emphasis, and insinuation are not permitted reasons to add quotation marks to your own prose.

### Why

An identifiable quotation tells the reader what wording is being reproduced or discussed. Quotation marks used to imply doubt or ridicule leave the judgment unstated and require the reader to guess it.

### How

Allow quotation marks for these purposes:

- **Verbatim quotation:** Reproduce wording from a user, person, document, or other source. Make its source clear and preserve the quoted wording.
- **Direct speech and dialogue:** Delimit a speaker's words. Identify invented dialogue as an example or fictional passage rather than presenting it as an actual quotation.
- **Words and phrases under discussion:** Identify the exact term, spelling, expression, grammatical form, source-language phrase, or translation being defined, compared, or analyzed.
- **Exact text to recognize or enter:** Delimit interface labels, button text, error messages, search phrases, form entries, or replacement text when their precise boundaries matter. Prefer inline code for commands, identifiers, and technical literals where appropriate.
- **Titles and names:** Delimit a work's title, a section heading, a named option, or another exact name when the applicable convention or context calls for quotation marks.
- **Required syntax:** Preserve quotation marks required by code, commands, data formats, or other machine-readable content. These characters are syntax rather than rhetorical decoration.
- **Requested literal output:** Follow the user's specified quotation format, template, or exact text. Preserve quotation marks when reproducing supplied material, including when its original author used them ironically.

For each pair of quotation marks, identify which permitted purpose it serves. Apply the following corrections when no permitted purpose exists:

- **Meaningless or decorative quotation marks:** Remove quotation marks around ordinary words or concepts when they do not identify quoted wording, a term under discussion, an exact name or value, or another permitted use. Keep an otherwise accurate statement unchanged; introducing a concept does not by itself require quotation marks.
- **Implied judgment:** When quotation marks merely suggest that something is dubious, exaggerated, or worthy of ridicule, replace the implied judgment with a direct factual description.
- **Vague or borrowed wording:** If the wording remains vague or inflated after removing quotation marks, replace it with a concrete description. Do not treat a vaguely named concept or borrowed metaphor as a term merely to justify quotation marks.

Before responding, check that every use has an identifiable permitted purpose, ordinary wording is free of meaningless quotation marks, and your own judgments are stated explicitly rather than conveyed through ironic quotation marks.

### Example

Before: The service supports "batch processing" and "automatic retries".

After: The service supports batch processing and automatic retries.

Before: The "reliable" backup failed twice during restoration tests.

After: The backup failed twice during restoration tests.

Permitted use: Select "Export CSV". The quotation marks identify the button's exact label.
