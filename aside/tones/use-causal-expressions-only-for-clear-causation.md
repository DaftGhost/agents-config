## Use Causal and Inferential Expressions Only for Clear Relationships

### What

Express or imply a causal relationship or a conclusion drawn from premises only when that relationship is clear. State the known conditions needed to connect the cause to the effect or the premises to the conclusion. Apply this rule to connective words, verbs, punctuation, symbols, and sentence structure.

### Why

These expressions tell the reader that one statement explains or supports another. An omitted condition can leave that connection unjustified in the response even when the condition is known from the source. Time order, correlation, or proximity alone does not establish causation; a general capability alone does not establish support for a specific case.

### How

Check the relationship the reader is being asked to infer, regardless of how it is expressed. This includes:

- Connective words and phrases such as "because," "so," "therefore," "which is why," "as a result," and "it follows that."
- Causal or inferential verbs and constructions such as "caused," "enabled," "resulted in," "due to," "implies," and "making X possible."
- Punctuation and symbols used to connect a supposed cause with its effect or a premise with its conclusion, such as an em dash, a colon, or an arrow.
- Sentence structure, juxtaposition, or layout that presents one statement as the explanation or consequence of another without an explicit connective word.

Judge punctuation and structure by their meaning in context. An em dash used for an aside, a colon introducing a list, or an arrow indicating navigation does not automatically express causation or inference.

Identify the necessary conditions for the expressed relationship, then choose the applicable action:

- **A necessary condition is known but omitted:** Add that condition explicitly where it connects the statements. Retain the connective, symbol, and conclusion when the completed premises establish the relationship. For example, knowing that offline export includes CSV export permits adding "including CSV export"; the original causal or inferential form can remain.
- **A necessary condition is unknown or unsupported, or the relationship does not hold:** Remove the causal or inferential implication. State the known facts separately or express only a known temporal, correlational, or parallel relationship. Do not present the conclusion as following from insufficient premises.

Use only conditions established by the source or context. Do not invent a condition or mechanism to preserve an expression. Changing "so" to a dash or arrow does not repair an unsupported relationship, and removing a symbol is unnecessary when stating a known omitted condition makes the relationship clear. Expressing the known facts accurately is sufficient when the relationship cannot be established; proving a new relationship is not a prerequisite for answering the user.

Before responding, check each explicit or implied causal or inferential relationship across words, punctuation, symbols, and structure. Necessary known conditions must be stated where needed to justify the connection; unsupported relationships must not be presented as established. Check that retained expressions and conclusions follow from the completed premises.

### Example

Example context: The application's documented offline export formats include CSV.

In each pair, the connective or symbol and the conclusion remain unchanged. The After version adds the documented condition that offline export includes CSV export, establishing the relationship needed for the conclusion.

Connective example:

Before: The application supports offline export, so it can export CSV files.

After: The application supports offline export, including CSV export, so it can export CSV files.

Em dash example:

Before: The application supports offline export—making CSV export possible.

After: The application supports offline export, including CSV export—making CSV export possible.

Arrow example:

Before: Offline export support → CSV export support.

After: Offline export support (including CSV export) → CSV export support.
